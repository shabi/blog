> *A small story about the moment when a person meets silence for the first time.*

---

## The Room Before Midnight

The first night was not special because something happened.

It was special because nothing happened.

I was twenty-seven years old when I arrived at that small apartment at the edge of the city. The room was unfamiliar, the walls were empty, and the only furniture that seemed to have a memory was an old wooden desk left by the previous owner.

Outside the window, the city continued moving.

Cars passed.

Lights disappeared.

People returned home.

But inside the room, there was only a strange and almost unbearable silence.

I had always believed that silence meant peace. Yet that night I discovered another truth:

**silence is sometimes a mirror.**

It does not comfort us immediately. It shows us what we have been avoiding.

## A Letter Never Sent

On the desk, I found a small envelope.

It was not addressed to anyone.

Inside was a single piece of paper with only one sentence:

> Every beginning looks empty before it becomes a memory.

I read the sentence several times.

At first, I thought it was meaningless. A forgotten phrase from someone whose name had already disappeared.

But as midnight approached, those words became heavier.

I began wondering:

- Who wrote this?
- Why did they leave it here?
- Was this room also their first night?
- Did they also sit alone and listen to the city breathing outside?

A room remembers more than people think.

The marks on the floor.

The small scratches on the desk.

The faded color of the wall.

They are all traces of invisible lives.

## The First Night of Being Alone

Before that night, I had always been surrounded by noise.

Messages.

Conversations.

Meetings.

The endless movement of daily life.

I thought those things were my life.

But when everything disappeared, I realized they were only the background of my life.

The true voice was much quieter.

It appeared only when nobody was speaking.

I wrote in my notebook:

> *关于一个人在陌生空间里第一次遇见自己的故事。*

## 午夜之前的房间

第一夜特别，并不是因为发生了什么。

恰恰相反，是因为**什么都没有发生**。

那一年，我二十七岁，第一次搬进城市边缘的一间小公寓。房间很陌生。墙壁是空白的。唯一带有一些过去痕迹的，是前任住客留下的一张旧木桌。它安静地靠在窗边，像一个已经等待了很久的旁观者。

窗外，城市依然运行。

车辆经过。

灯光熄灭。

人们回到各自的家。

可是房间里面，只剩下一种陌生而无法忽视的安静。

我曾经以为，安静意味着平和。

但那一夜，我第一次明白：

**安静有时候是一面镜子。**

它不会立即安慰我们。

它只是让我们看见那些一直被忽略的自己。

## 一封没有寄出的信

桌上放着一个小小的信封。

没有名字。

没有地址。

里面只有一张折叠起来的纸。

上面写着一句话：

> 每一个开始，在成为回忆之前，看起来都是空白。

我读了很多遍。

最开始，我觉得这句话没有特别的意义。

也许只是某个人随手留下的一句话。

也许写下它的人，早已经忘记了这里。

可是随着午夜越来越近，那句话开始变得沉重。

我开始想：

- 写下它的人是谁？
- 为什么要留下它？
- 这间房间是否也是他的第一夜？
- 他是否也曾独自坐在这里，听着城市缓慢呼吸？

房间比人们想象中记得更多。

地板上的痕迹。

桌面的划痕。

墙壁褪去的颜色。

它们都是那些无法被记录的生命留下的证明。

## 第一次独处的夜晚

在那之前，我一直生活在声音之中。

消息。

交谈。

会议。

永不停歇的日常。

我曾经以为，那些就是我的生活。

可是当所有声音消失以后，我才发现：

它们只是生活的背景。

真正属于自己的声音，总是更加安静。

它只有在没有人说话的时候才会出现。

我打开笔记本，写下一段话：

```text
第一夜并不是我们抵达某个地方的夜晚。

第一夜，是我们终于遇见自己的时刻。
```
